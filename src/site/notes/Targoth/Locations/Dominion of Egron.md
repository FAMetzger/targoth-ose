###### **Name:**
Dominion of Egron


###### **Capital:**  
Egron has no permanent capital. Instead, once each year, the clans converge on the shores of **Lake Maroonkum**, a deep, iron-dark body of water fed by hidden springs and believed to be older than the sky itself. The lake is sacred: no blood may be spilled within sight of its waters, and no weapon may be drawn in anger.  

Here, at the **Great Gathering**, disputes are settled, marriages arranged, raids sanctioned or forbidden, and a **High Khan of the Dominion** is chosen or reaffirmed. The High Khan’s authority lasts only so long as they can command loyalty—once the Gathering ends, power returns to the steppe, and obedience is enforced by reputation rather than law.

At present, the clans are divided. Rival khans feud endlessly, and no single leader has yet unified the Dominion beyond the ritual authority granted at Maroonkum. Many elders whisper that the steppe itself is waiting for a conqueror.


###### **Description:** 
The Dominion of Egron sprawls across immense wind-rolled steppe, broken by low hills, rocky escarpments, and wide river valleys that freeze solid in winter and flood in spring. Grasses grow thick and tough, perfect for grazing, while the horizon seems endless beneath a vast, merciless sky. Forests are rare, clustered only along distant borders, and stone is scarce—Egron is a land that favors tents over walls and horses over roads.

The people of Egron live as clan-based nomads, bound by blood, oath, and tradition rather than territory. Families travel in wagon trains and felt yurts, following ancient seasonal routes known as Sky-Paths. Leadership within clans is earned through strength, generosity, and success in war or trade, not inheritance alone. Old grudges linger for generations, and alliances are as fluid as the grasslands themselves.

Egron’s wealth is measured in living things. The Dominion’s chief exports are horses without equal, bred for endurance, speed, and hardiness. Egron mounts are prized by every neighboring power and often fetch absurd prices in silver or steel.

Caravans crossing the steppe do so only with Egron guides or heavy tribute. Trade routes are not marked by roads but by memory and song.

###### **Other:**
To settled kingdoms, the Dominion of Egron is a paradox:

- **Feared**, for its lightning-fast riders who can strike without warning and vanish into the grasslands
    
- **Respected**, for its strict codes of hospitality and oath-keeping once terms are agreed
    
- **Frustrating**, as no embassy ever speaks for all of Egron, and treaties die when the Gathering disperses
