###### **Name:** 
Gundabuz

###### **Capital:** 
Gundabuz “capital” is not a city but a **moving convergence of clans** known as the **Crowncamp**. Several times a year, predetermined routes and celestial signs guide the Trail-Houses to gather on wide, defensible plains. When assembled, thousands of wagons form a colossal ring-city, with the **High Pavilion of the Crown** at its center.

###### **Description:** 
Gundabuz sprawls across vast high plains that fade northward into wind-scoured tundra, a land of endless sky and hard horizons. Summer brings brief, golden grasslands alive with migrating herds; winter freezes the world into iron, locking rivers beneath ice and driving life southward. Low, broken hills and ancient glacial scars offer little shelter, and permanent stone cities are all but impossible to maintain. Survival here belongs to those who move.

 
The folk of Gundabuz are nomadic by necessity and tradition. Families belong to extended clans called Trail-Houses, traveling with massive wagon-circles drawn by herd beasts and reinforced sleds in winter. Tents of felt, hide, and treated canvas can be raised or struck within an hour. Even births, marriages, trials, and funerals occur on the move.  
Despite this mobility, Gundabuz possess a strong sense of law and identity—oral codes memorized by every adult, recited around night fires to ensure they are never lost.

Gundabuz's wealth walks on four legs. Enormous herds of cold-hardened beasts—broad-backed elk-oxen, tundra sheep, and long-horned grazer kine—are driven along seasonal routes. These animals are prized abroad for their meat, wool, hides, bone, and fat, all resistant to cold and rot.  
Along the southern border, the plains give way to dense boreal forests. Here, semi-permanent camps and river-mills process lumber harvested by clan crews who rotate in shifts, ensuring no group becomes tied to the land. Timber is floated south in great log-rafts, traded to settled kingdoms for grain, metalwork, and luxuries Gundabuz cannot produce.


###### **Ruling Structure:**
**Way-King or Way-Queen**, elected for life by clan elders and confirmed through ritual endurance rather than bloodline alone. When the Crowncamp moves, so does the heart of the kingdom—treasury wagons, archives etched into bone and wood, and even sacred landmarks mounted on sleds.

###### **Other:**

To the north lie the [[Targoth/Locations/Norrhollow\|Norrhollow]] home to fiercely warlike peoples who thrive in the dead tundra and ice-choked fjords. These raiders believe strength is proven only through conquest and bloodshed. Gundabuzk’s open lands make tempting targets, and border skirmishes are constant.  
Rather than fixed fortresses, Gundabuz relies on moving defense: outrider scouts, signal fires carried on wagons, and rapid mustering of mounted warriors who fight in fluid formations, striking hard and vanishing into the plains.

Military & Defense  
Every adult Gundabuzian is expected to fight. Warriors favor bows, spears, and hooked lances, ideal for mounted combat and herd defense. Armor is layered hide and lacquered wood—light, warm, and easily repaired. When the Crowncamp is threatened, the wagons themselves become walls, chained together and bristling with archers.

Reputation Beyond Its Borders  
Southern kingdoms see Gundabuz as rugged but reliable traders—people who honor bargains fiercely but disdain walls and thrones. Northerners curse them as elusive prey that refuses to stand and die properly.  

Among bards, Gundabuz is known as _“the kingdom that leaves no footprints,”_ a nation that survives not by holding land, but by knowing when to move—and when to run no more.
