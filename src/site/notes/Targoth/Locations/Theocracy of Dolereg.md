###### **Name:** 
Theocracy of Dolereg

###### **Capital:** 
Penitent Marches

###### **Description:** 
The Theocracy of Dolereg is a rigid theocracy ruled by the **High Hierophant** , believed by the faithful to be the sole living mouthpiece of the Tiamet. Beneath them sits a lattice of Prelate-Governors, Abbots of the Tithe, and militant Field-Priests, each wielding both spiritual and temporal authority. Law and doctrine are indistinguishable; heresy is not merely sin but treason. Sacred texts are interpreted literally, and new interpretations are forbidden without a formal revelation—something that has not occurred in generations.

**Economy: The Holy Estates**  
The Theocracy of Dolereg’s economy is almost entirely agrarian, dominated by vast **Consecrated Estates** owned by the Church. These estates stretch for miles—grain fields, orchards, and livestock pens worked by “**the Devoted**,” a class of bound laborers officially described as pilgrims working off spiritual debts. In practice, they are little more than slaves.  

All produce flows upward: first to the local abbey granaries, then to the Great Storehouses of the capital. Excess grain is traded abroad through tightly controlled caravans, making the Theocracy of Dolereg wealthy in coin but austere in daily life. Independent farmers technically exist, but crushing tithes and mandatory “voluntary service” inevitably force them into Church estates within a generation.

**Society & Daily Life**  
Life is harsh and monotonous. Bells dictate every hour—prayer, labor, prayer again. Literacy is restricted to clergy and scribes, as the Church teaches that untrained minds invite blasphemous interpretations. Families are communal, assigned housing near the fields they work. Children are tested at a young age for “Purity of Will”; those who show zeal or obedience are taken to be raised by the Church, never to return.  


**The Faith Militant & Raids for “Conversion”**  
The **Scythe Legions**, warrior-acolytes,  are officially, charged with the defense of the sacred borders and escort tithe caravans. Unofficially, they conduct brutal raids into neighboring lands.  
These raids are framed as **Redemptive Harvests**—the seizure of unbelievers to be “saved from damnation.” Entire villages have vanished overnight, their inhabitants marched in chains back to the Theocracy of Dolereg, baptized by force, and assigned to the estates. Diplomatic protests are dismissed as the whining of the spiritually lost.

**Rumors of Corruption**  
Despite its obsession with purity, whispers of rot fester beneath the Theocracy of Dolereg’s piety:

- Certain High Abbots live in hidden luxury, feasting on rare imports while preaching holy deprivation.
    
- Tithes are rumored to be falsified, with grain quietly diverted to secret markets or hoarded against an unnamed prophesied calamity.
    
- Some claim the High Hierophant has not spoken with Tiamet at all, but is advised by a secret inner council—perhaps even something non-human or infernal masquerading as divine guidance.
    
- A forbidden rumor persists that the faith’s central miracle—the annual blessing that ensures bountiful harvests—requires blood sacrifice, not prayer.
    

**Reputation Beyond Its Borders**  
To the rest of the world, the Theocracy of Dolereg is a nightmare clothed in righteousness: a land of endless fields watered by sweat and fear, where hymns mask chains. Merchants trade with it cautiously, diplomats lie through their teeth, and border lords build watchtowers facing its lands.
